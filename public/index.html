    How it works

    Three parties. Only the owner can take money out.

    Agent key

    A function-call key on the vault, limited to supply, withdraw and claim. It cannot send NEAR, call tokens or touch owner methods.

    Vault contract

    Approved venues with fixed contracts, a cap per move and per venue, a cooldown, a daily limit, and withdrawals only to the owner.

    Risk feeder

    Blocks a venue or pauses the vault when an exploit or depeg lands. It cannot unblock, add venues or move funds.

    FAQ

    What if the agent's key is stolen?
    The thief gets the agent's powers and nothing more: moving capped amounts between the vault and venues the owner approved. NEAR refuses any transfer or method outside the key's list before it runs, and the contract refuses the rest. The owner or risk feeder can delete the key in one call.
    Why does the highest yield get a smaller allocation?
    Rhea Lend pays the most on NEAR, and it lost $18.4M to price manipulation on 16 April 2026 with no funds returned. Its pools are also about 89% lent out, so only a slice can leave at short notice. The policy keeps the venue and cuts the size.
    Is the yield real?
    Lending rates come from the Rhea Lend contract and are paid by borrowers; the reward farms are empty. Staking rates come from the change in the LiNEAR and rNEAR share price over the last 30 days.
    Which network is the vault on?
    The vault and drill shown here run on NEAR testnet against the testnet deployments of Burrow (the code Rhea Lend runs) and LiNEAR. The yield table reads mainnet.
    Is the code open?
    Yes, MIT licensed: the Rust vault contract, the agent and policy, and the drill.
