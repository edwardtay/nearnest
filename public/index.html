      My vault

      Create your own NEARnest vault on testnet. You own it. The agent gets one function-call key on it and nothing more, and every rule on this page applies to your money too.

      1. Connect a testnet wallet. No account yet? Create one in Meteor Wallet and switch it to testnet; new testnet accounts come with test NEAR. (MyNearWallet has stopped signing transactions.)
      2. Create your vault. One transaction to the factory makes <name>.factory, deploys the vault, adds the agent's key and makes you the owner. Your deposit pays the vault's storage and stays in the vault.
      3. Fund it with test USDC from the public faucet token, and NEAR to stake.
      4. Watch the agent work within your vault's rules, and use the owner buttons any time: withdraw to yourself, pause, remove the agent's key.

                  How it works

                  Three parties. Only the owner can take money out.

                  Agent key

                  A function-call key on the vault, limited to supply, withdraw and claim. It cannot send NEAR, call tokens or touch owner methods.

                  Vault contract

                  Approved venues with fixed contracts, a cap per move and per venue, a cooldown, a daily limit, and withdrawals only to the owner. Positions are read back from each venue after every exit.

                  Risk feeder

                  Lowers a venue's cap to the policy's size, blocks a venue, pauses the vault, deletes the agent key, and pulls funds back into the vault. It cannot raise a cap, unblock, or send funds anywhere else.

                  FAQ

                  What if the agent's key is stolen?
                  The thief gets the agent's powers and nothing more: moving capped amounts between the vault and venues the owner approved. NEAR refuses any transfer or method outside the key's list before it runs, and the contract refuses the rest. The policy's tighter caps are on chain too: the risk feeder sets them and can only lower them. The owner or risk feeder can delete the key in one call and still pull funds out of venues without it.
                  Why does the highest yield get a smaller allocation?
                  Rhea Lend pays the most on NEAR, and it lost $18.4M to price manipulation on 16 April 2026; about $11.2M was later returned or frozen (The Block, 17 April). Its USDC pool is also about 87% lent out (9 November 2026), so only a slice can leave at short notice; the live figure is on the Yield tab. The policy keeps the venue and cuts the size to half.
                  Is the yield real?
                  Lending rates come from the Rhea Lend contract and are paid by borrowers; the reward farms are empty. Staking rates come from the change in the LiNEAR and rNEAR share price over the last 30 days.
                  Which network is the vault on?
                  Both. The page opens on the mainnet vault, owned by web3wagmi.near, which holds real USDC and NEAR in Rhea Lend and LiNEAR under small caps. Switch to Testnet in the header for the demo vault, the 28-check drill and the factory; they run against the testnet deployments of Burrow (the code Rhea Lend runs) and LiNEAR, with test tokens of no value. The yield table always reads mainnet, and the testnet agent sizes its moves from those mainnet judgements.
